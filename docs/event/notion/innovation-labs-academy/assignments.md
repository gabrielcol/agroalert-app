# Assignments

**This is what gets reviewed before Producthon:** hypotheses list and interview questions deliverables. The interview outcomes and personas are optional, but they will be your unfair advantage.

Due by 10 September | Team-level submission

---

#### 1. Hypotheses

A hypothesis says: _our proposed solution solves [problem] with [solution]._ Unproven until a real customer validates it.

**Requirements:**

1. Minimum 10 hypotheses
1. Each one is testable so you can walk into an interview and come out with a clear validated / invalidated
1. Each one is clear and specific with no vague statements
1. Covers more than one area: product, customer, channel, pricing, market, competition, feasibility
1. Run through the Riskiest-Assumption Matrix. What is important + no evidence is your riskiest zone
   Don't write hypotheses trying to confirm what you already believe. That's lying to yourself, and it shows later.

#### 2. Interview Guide

Written questions, prepped, ready to run — not something you improvise on the spot.

**Requirements:**

1. Written down questions
1. Open-ended questions only. Avoid what’s answerable with yes/no
1. No leading questions and if they answer exactly what you expected, rewrite it
1. Piloted on a friendlier person first, before using it on a real target customer
1. Built to validate the problem space. Do not confirm one narrow feature
   Based on _The Mom Test_.

#### 3. Interview Outcome

It is important to validate your ideas, to talk to people. Reach out to them on LinkedIn, find them in your circles and just be curious about their takes, experiences and problems.

Requirements:

1. Notes or a recording for every interview (we do not encourage reconstructing from memory)
1. A transcript or summary per interview
1. Patterns clustered across interviews (recurring pains, contradictions, surprises)
1. Each insight marked validated / invalidated against your hypotheses list
1. Include the ones that hurt — an invalidated hypothesis is a result, not a failure to hide

> Remember that you're not collecting approval, you're collecting reality. Write down what confused you as much as what confirmed you.

#### 4. Personas

Your target customer as a fictional representation of a type of person — built from what you heard, not who you imagine.

[Here’s the template you can download](https://www.notion.so/p/3ceb2fbebae980a3a82ec5202d266e8c?pvs=25#3ceb2fbebae980efb1a6c201146ab611)

Requirements:

1. Build 1, 2, or 3 personas. Your call, based on how many distinct roles your interviews surfaced
1. Every trait traces back to something a real person told you
1. For each persona, cover: current approach (how they solve this today, what it actually costs them), needs/pains/goals, and their bigger purpose
1. If more than one persona exists, note the restriction between them: who controls whose actions (e.g. kid uses it, parent pays)
1. Prioritize early adopters: build for the segment covering most of your user base, not the extremes you'll never satisfy

> If you can't trace a trait back to an interview, that is a [Proto Persona](https://www.notion.so/p/3ceb2fbebae980a3a82ec5202d266e8c?pvs=25#3ceb2fbebae9800aabdee9d0548f5d54). You can build it with article data or public interviews
