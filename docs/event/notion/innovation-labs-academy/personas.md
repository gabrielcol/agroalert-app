# Personas

A persona = your target customer as a **fictional representation** of a type of person. Not "Gigel from Bucharest, someone's friend" - an _actor_ who represents everyone with a specific set of characteristics.

**They answer one question:** _**Who are we building this product for?**_

They're the light you do surgery under - _"the single most powerful design tool"_ (Alan Cooper). The goal is **empathy + prioritizing user needs.**

#### **→ Most products have multiple personas**

They usually have **restrictions between each other** (one controls another's actions):

- Tool for kids → kids don't pay → add **parents**. Kids under 10 vs. over 10 → two personas. Add education → add **teachers**.
- Employees with no budget → the real buyer is the **institution**.
  Build and talk to _all_ of them.

#### **→ Questions to build a persona**

- **Age group / who actually does the target actions?**
- **Current approach** - how do they solve this today? How much does it _really_ cost them?
- **Why this existing model?** Why nothing simpler? Did they try and fail, not know how, not have the money/tools, not want to install it?
- **Common needs, pains, goals** - what do they actually want (in general, not from your product)?
- **Purpose** - the _bigger why_. A parent buying a kids' app is investing in a bigger purpose, not just the app.
  Understand the **whole problem space** and where you fit into their overall purpose.

#### **→ Example - one shopping app, two personas**

- **Michael:** tech-comfortable. Compares price ranges, splits delivery costs with friends, optimizes cost, wants nutrition info (into sports), loves promotions.
- **Josh:** less tech-experience, phone only. Can't compare shops/products, doesn't want products chosen for him, wants a local-products filter.
  If you only build for one, only that one uses it. Cater to both or lose the other.

#### **→ Focus: early adopters, not the extremes**

There are **extreme personas** at the edge of your user base you'll never fully satisfy. At the start, **focus on the personas covering most of your base - your early adopters** - and build the MVP for them. Don't chase the extremes who might never buy.

#### **→ Supporting tool: Day-in-the-Life / Empathy Map**

Map, across a day/week/month: what they **think & feel** (pains & gains), what they **need to do**, what they **hear** (info coming their way, what friends/colleagues say), what they **do**, **see**, and **observe**. Real day-in-life = _"I wake up, make coffee, drive to work, take calls, answer emails, lunch, meetings…"_ - map who they interact with and everything they experience.

#### **→ Quantitative vs. Qualitative**

- **Surveys (quantitative)** are fine but weak alone - people answer whatever, you can't see or probe them. Offer free ice cream and they'll say what they think you want to hear → **bias.**
- **Interviews (qualitative)** - open questions let them express _why_. _"How did you feel when you saw the free ice cream? Why? Where had you seen that before?"_

#### **→ Where AI fits in personas - and where it doesn't**

![](images/ce55b91ab211.jpg)

Replace users with AI and you build something **fictional.** AI gives you answers, not _understanding_. That's why every activity puts you in front of real people - so reality hits you early instead of years in.
