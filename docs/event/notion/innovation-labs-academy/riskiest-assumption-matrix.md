# Riskiest-Assumption Matrix

**Instructions:**
Below are 8 assumptions behind a fictional product, **Fitly** — a scheduling and booking tool for boutique gym owners.

Plot each one on the matrix: Importance (low/high) on one axis, Evidence (low/high) on the other.

Anything landing in "important, no evidence" is your riskiest zone. Your placement doesn't have to match the reference exactly — check your reasoning against two questions: is this actually make-or-break for the business, and do we have anything besides a guess behind it.

**Content:**

1. Gym owners currently manage class schedules manually via Instagram or WhatsApp.
1. Gym owners will pay €30/month for the tool.
1. Gym owners want a mobile app rather than a desktop dashboard.
1. Members will actually book classes themselves instead of calling the gym.
1. Competitors already offer similar scheduling automation, and gym owners know about them.
1. The tool can integrate with Instagram's booking API within 2 months.
1. Gym owners will switch tools even if it means retraining staff.
1. The app's color scheme matters to gym owners' brand perception.
