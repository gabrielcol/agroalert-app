# Customer Interviews

We encourage **interviews**, not focus groups (you can't run enough) and not surveys (too leading-a Facebook form is not customer discovery). **You need face-to-face conversations.**

**The best interviews are 90% listening and 10% talking.**

**The classic anti-pattern:** the founder who tests his idea on his parents. They'll love it-they're your parents; they _have_ to. That's not validation.

#### **→ Before the interview**

- **Identify what you want to learn.** Early on you run **exploratory interviews-validate** the whole _problem space_, not one narrow feature.
- **Prepare a guide.** Written-down questions. Seem prepared and respect their time.
- **Pilot the guide** on a friendlier person first.
- **Record it.** Bring a colleague to take notes, or record + transcribe. (You can now transcribe + summarize with AI)

#### **→ During the interview - social cues**

These build rapport so people actually open up (people don't answer openly if you're not friendly):

- **Eye contact**
- **Nodding** - signals you're listening and following
- **Mirroring** - they sip water, you sip water; they nod, you nod (works on dates too, apparently)
- **Echoing** - repeat the last few words of their sentence; people feel heard and keep going
  You need them talking **90% of the time**, with all their problems on the table.

**Everyone lies** - not maliciously. The first things people say are what make them feel good or get rid of you faster. Dig deeper. Make it feel like they're telling a friend about their job.

#### **→ The Schindler example (why environment beats features)**

Building a tiny app for **elevator technicians** - guys hanging in a dark elevator shaft, thick gloves, an iPhone 5. The job wasn't "add features." It was **understanding the environment** the app would be used in. Miss that and the app is useless no matter how good the features are.

#### **→ Where AI fits in interviews - and where it doesn't**

**Use AI to:**

- Validate or generate interview questions (as a UX researcher / PM, open-ended, no leading, focused on behavior - you can point it at _The Mom Test_)
- Simulate interview scenarios to know what to expect
- Structure interview notes; extract patterns across interviews (though if you were _in_ the room, your mind already caught them)
  **Do NOT use AI to:**

- Replace talking to real users (**we check that you talked to 10**)
- Generate fake user insights - it only hurts _you_ later
- Validate your idea - **AI won't use your product; people will**
- Decide what users need without asking users
