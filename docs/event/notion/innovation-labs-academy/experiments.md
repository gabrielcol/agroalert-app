# Experiments

An experiment tests different ideas/approaches to find what works - _before_ you make a heavy investment.

**Why run them:**

- Reduce the risk of building the wrong thing
- Validate assumptions with **data-driven insight**
- Improve the experience
- Prevent **solution-first thinking** - force problem validation first
  **The flow:** define objective + hypothesis → design the experiment → collect & analyze data → iterate & learn.

**Example (with variables):**

_If we reduce the number of buttons (independent variable), then we'll get more registrations (dependent variable)._ Same logic as making a button red vs. green to test clicks.

#### **→ Key concepts**

- **Hypothesis-driven** - every experiment starts with a testable hypothesis
- **Independent vs. dependent variables** - what you _change_ vs. what you _measure_
- **Minimum Viable Experiment (MVE)** - the smallest test that gives you actionable data. Aim for this; you don't have money to burn on huge experiments.
- **Avoid bias** - interviews are experiments too, and bias sneaks in fast

### **⚙️ 5. Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)**

A tool for developing products by looking at the **job the customer is trying to get done** - and the process that leads them to "hire" a product to do it.

Map three things:

1. **Current state** - how things are in the user's life today
1. **Outcome** - them using your product
1. **The progress between the two** - _this is the part people underestimate_
   If the **progress** (switching cost, effort, setup) is too hard, they won't make the change - even if your product genuinely would help. That friction is often _why_ they don't adopt. Take it seriously.
