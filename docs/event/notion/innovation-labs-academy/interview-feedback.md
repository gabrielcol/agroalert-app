# Interview Feedback

**Instructions:**

This is a short exercise built around an interview transcript: a founder testing an invoicing tool with a freelance graphic designer.

We're doing this because writing a good interview question is harder than it looks, and most people can't tell a leading question from a real one until they see it fail in someone else's mouth.

You'll switch hats twice: first you're the interviewee, answering all six questions yourself and rating each one as you go. Then you put on the mentor hat, read how the real interviewee actually answered, and rate the same six questions again — this time judging the question by the answer it actually produced.

| #   | Question                                                         |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Do you think an automated invoicing tool would help you?         |
| 2   | Walk me through the last time you sent an invoice to a client.   |
| 3   | Wouldn't it save time if you didn't have to chase late payments? |
| 4   | How do you currently track which invoices are overdue?           |
| 5   | Would you pay €10 a month for an automated system?               |
| 6   | What's the most annoying part of getting paid as a freelancer?   |

Do the following:

1. Answer: Get in the graphic designer freelancer's shoes and reply to the questions
1. Rate: Rate the questions you received. Are they good enough for an interview?
1. Reveal: See responses from an interview transcript
1. Re-rate: Think once again. Are they good enough? What is missing?
1. Reflection: Which ratings changed between the two rounds? Did writing your own answer already tip you off that a question was bad — or did it only become obvious once you saw someone else's answer?
